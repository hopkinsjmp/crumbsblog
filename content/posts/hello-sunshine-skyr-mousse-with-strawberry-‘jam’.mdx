ingredients: |-
  150g ripe strawberries, plus extra for garnish
  2 tsp chia seeds
  2 regular egg whites
  150g Skyr (or swap out for mascarpone for an indulgent treat)
  1 tsp vanilla essence
  2 tsp honey (or sweetener of choice), to taste
  Flaked almonds, for garnish
method: |-
  1. Hull the strawberries and slice in half. Put into a bowl and mash with a fork until they are a spoonable consistency; don’t worry about mashing into a smooth mixture. 
  2. Add 1 tsp honey (or sweetener of choice). Now fork in the chia seeds, scraping any from the sides of the bowl. Set aside for 10 minutes so that it thickens into a ‘jam’ (courtesy of the seeds).
  3. While the strawberries are standing, separate the eggs, plopping the egg whites into a large clean and dry bowl. Save the yolks in the fridge to make extra-rich scrambled eggs (be sure to add 1-2 extra eggs, though).
  4. Now whisk, either by hand or with an electric beater, until stiff peaks form. This should take up to 5 or 2 minutes respectively.
  5. Measure out the Skyr into a fresh large bowl and mix with a spoon to soften it up a little. Now add a little of the whisked egg whites and mix again so that the Skyr takes on a much more malleable consistency.
  6. Mix in the vanilla essence and remaining 1 tsp of honey (or sweetener of choice). 
  7. With a metal spoon, carefully fold the remaining egg whites into the Skyr until you have a smooth and aerated texture. Taste for sweetness and vanilla, adding more if you think it needs any.
  8. In a vessel of your choosing, layer first the strawberry ‘jam’ and then the Skyr. Chill in the fridge for 4 hours or overnight.
  9. When you’re ready to eat it, garnish with strawberry slices and almond flakes (briefly toasted in a dry pan). Dive in with a little spoon as you relocate from your kitchen to your final dining destination.
storage: Eat within 2-3 days.

Yes, I know, it’s been a while since I posted any new recipes. What was meant to be a fortnight’s break turned into triple that. What was I doing during this time? Well, obviously not writing. Like everyone, I was navigating the world today, i.e. trying to conjure a career into existence and figure out ‘what I want to do when I grow up’. 

Oh, and I went to India for a wedding. During which I learnt: 1) everything is spicy, even the ‘not spicy’ option; 2) shaking one’s head seems to mean everything but no; 3) do not presume to have a superior gut microbiome: you will s\*\*\* yourself.

Having fully recovered from the trip, I now feel physically capable to dive back into the food, flavours and culinary fusions that excite me. And, at the moment, I’m all about whipping up dishes that feel effortlessly summery: the edible equivalent to throwing on a beach kaftan. 

So, on the subject of ‘whipping’ and following on from my last post, I continue on the subject of strawberries. They’re still widely available and, right now, increasingly a little too ripe. 

This recipe was borne out of wanting a healthy yet indulgent summer-time breakfast. I use Skyr, an icelandic protein-rich yoghurt. On its own, I find it a little too claggy to enjoy—regardless of the health benefits it claims to have. However, whipped up in this way, with equal parts refreshingly soft-set strawberries, it strikes the perfect balanced ‘mouth-feel’. 

The recipe makes enough for two, on the assumption that you want your weekend mornings covered. You could also share them, of course, but it’s not like me to suggest that in the first instance. 

It also doubles up exceptionally as an after-dinner treat, when the Skyr is replaced with the same amount of mascarpone (but be warned, this is for those who loooove creamy desserts). See below for more suggestions on how to make it more breakfast or dessert-’conscious’.

**A few notes on the recipe:** 

As with almost all my breakfast recipes, this one is primed to be adapted (see [_On The Matter of Breakfast Toppings_](https://crumbsofsanity.com/posts/on-the-matter-of-breakfast-toppings/) for ideas). Some initial suggestions I’d have is: adding rosewater to the strawberries (just a tad, to avoid soapy notes), and you could of course swap out the strawberry ‘jam’ for the [_Rose, Rhubarb and Strawberry Delight_](https://crumbsofsanity.com/posts/rose-rhubarb-and-strawberry-delight/) compote in my last post. For toppings, cacao nibs or dark chocolate shards—ever a good accompaniment to strawberries—would also work well, as would toasted pumpkin seeds (I do love the pink, green and white hues together) or granola. 

If you wanted to make this with mascarpone instead, consider scraping out a vanilla pod and dispensing the seeds into the mixture instead of using vanilla essence. I also wouldn’t dismiss the thought of having the mascarpone version for breakfast. According to ChatGPT—I left math behind with geography at secondary school—the calorie content would be equivalent to most typical breakfasts (porridge, Greek yoghurt, scrambled eggs on toast, eggs benedict, etc.) but, I hear you, the fat content may still be a little too high for it to be considered a serious breakfast contender. If you agree, or want a toned-down dessert, try halving the ingredients for the mascarpone layer so that it presents as a luscious upper crust and not a rich mantle.\*

\*Yes, I do feel compelled to point out my geography pun.
